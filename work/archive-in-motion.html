Real-time digitisation of intangible cultural heritage — converting oral traditions into navigable 3D archives that outlive their carriers.

Commissioned by Heritage Malta and developed across twelve months of field research, Archive in Motion addresses a critical gap in cultural preservation methodology: the intangible — song, gesture, oral testimony — resists conventional archiving. This work proposes a new model, one in which the act of documentation is itself made visible and navigable.

Concept

Against the Static Archive

Traditional heritage archives fix culture at a single moment, stripping it of the relational and contextual qualities that give it meaning. Archive in Motion proposes a counter-model: a living repository in which oral testimonies, folk gestures, and musical phrases are spatially embedded in a navigable 3D environment reconstructed from photogrammetric scans of the sites where they were collected.

Visitors navigate this environment in real time, approaching spatial anchors that trigger layered media — a grandmother singing a lullaby, the memory of a harvest rhythm, a fisherman describing how water sounds at different depths. The archive breathes; it has temperature and direction.

Process

Field Research & Real-Time Construction

The production process unfolded in three phases. First, an ethnographic fieldwork period across six months, conducting recorded interviews and performance documentation with communities across Malta's southern villages. Second, photogrammetric scanning of the primary sites — farmsteads, coastal fishing piers, a disused salt pan — using drone and ground-level rigs. Third, construction of the interactive environment in Unreal Engine 5, with Nanite geometry and Lumen lighting to preserve the fidelity of the scanned environments.

The interactive layer was designed to be controlled by a museum attendant using a simplified interface, allowing the archive to be demonstrated as a group experience or navigated individually by informed visitors.

Context

Preservation as Practice

The installation premiered at MUZA, Valletta, and subsequently toured to the Mdina Digital Arts Festival. Feedback from heritage professionals attending both venues centred on the potential of the model to be extended to other cultural contexts — a conversation that has since led to ongoing discussions about a follow-up commission in partnership with institutions in Sicily and Tunisia.
