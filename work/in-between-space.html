An installation inhabiting threshold zones — doorways, corridors, margins — as sites of encounter, hesitation, and passage.

Architecture typically organises space into categories: here is a room for this, there is a corridor for passing through. In Between Space proposes that the most interesting things happen in the moments between categories — in doorways, in passages, in the edges that separate designated spaces. These threshold zones are ordinarily treated as non-spaces, places you move through without dwelling. The installation refuses this logic.

Sound and light are deployed in the transitional zones of the host building — corridors, stairwells, vestibules — drawing visitors into them and encouraging a slowed, attentive movement through spaces that are normally passed without thought. The sonic material changes as visitors progress: resonances shift, densities vary, the texture of sound mirrors the texture of the space.

The work invites a phenomenological relationship with built space — an attention to how architecture feels before it is identified, how the body registers scale, material, and light before the mind has catalogued the room.
