A gamified walkthrough of a sixteenth-century fortress — mixed-reality overlays and location-based triggers make four hundred years of history simultaneously present and playable.

Commissioned by Heritage Malta and installed permanently at Fort St Elmo, Valletta, Play / Pause transforms a historic military fortification into a layered mixed-reality game environment. Visitors receive a device at the entrance; as they move through the fort's spaces, location-based triggers reveal historical layers — a seventeenth-century garrison at drill, an eighteenth-century cannonball trajectory, a wartime radio operator composing a dispatch — overlaid on the physical space in real time.

Concept

History as Game Mechanic

The framing of heritage as game is not a trivialisation but a reclamation of engagement. Conventional museum interpretation asks visitors to observe; Play / Pause asks them to act — to navigate, to trigger, to choose. The game mechanics are deliberately light: there are no scores, no failure states. The player's task is simply to be present in the right place at the right time, and the reward is history made visible.

The title refers to the temporal duality the work constructs: the visitor simultaneously inhabits the present tense (walking through a contemporary tourist site) and presses play on the past (triggering historical scenes). Pausing means stopping, looking, dwelling — an act the work actively encourages through spatial design of the trigger zones.

Process

Location-Based XR & 3D Reconstruction

The technical foundation is a Unity XR application using GPS and compass heading for outdoor positioning, supplemented by ArUco marker tracking in interior spaces where satellite signal is unreliable. Historical scenes were constructed from a combination of 3D modelling, photogrammetric reconstruction of surviving artefacts, and archival illustration — with character animation drawn from motion capture sessions with a small ensemble of performers.

Historical content was developed in close collaboration with Heritage Malta's curatorial team, with all scenes reviewed for historical accuracy before final implementation. The audio layer — narration, ambient sound reconstruction, and period-appropriate music — was recorded in Maltese and English, with subtitles for accessibility.

Context

Permanent Installation & Reach

Since its installation in 2022, Play / Pause has been experienced by over 40,000 visitors to Fort St Elmo, making it one of the most-visited interactive heritage installations in Malta. Heritage Malta has reported a significant increase in average visitor dwell time at the fort since the installation's launch.
