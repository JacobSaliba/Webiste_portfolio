A live performance in which the body becomes an instrument — motion capture data routed directly into spatial audio arrays, turning movement into sound and sound into visible form.

Resonant Field is a fifty-minute live performance for one performer, a sixteen-speaker spatial audio array, and generative visuals. The performer wears a full-body motion capture suit; their movement is tracked in real time and mapped to audio parameters — velocity to pitch, acceleration to density, position to spatial location — so that the sound field is directly authored by the body's motion through space.

Concept

The Body as Score

Conventional musical performance separates the production of sound from the body that produces it — a pianist uses their fingers, a violinist their arm, a vocalist their throat. Resonant Field collapses this separation: the entire body, moving through space, becomes the instrument. There is no instrument interposed between body and sound.

The piece draws on the theoretical lineage of embodied cognition — the idea that cognition and sensation are not separable from the body and its environment. In this context, the performer does not think about what sound to make; they move, and the sound emerges as a direct consequence of that movement. The score is not notated; it is choreographed, and the choreography is improvised.

Sound Design

Spatial Audio Architecture

Audio synthesis runs in Max/MSP, receiving OSC data from the motion capture system at 120 frames per second. A bank of sixteen independent synthesis channels — each mapped to a speaker in the surrounding array — allows sound to be positioned and moved through the performance space with sub-second latency. The synthesis palette spans granular textures, physical modelling, and spectral processing, giving the performer a range from near-silence to dense polyphony.

The visual layer, rendered in Notch and driven by the same motion data, projects onto three large surfaces at the rear and sides of the stage — abstract forms that respond to sound density and performer position, creating a spatial audiovisual environment that envelops the audience.

Context

Performance & Tour

The work premiered at Teatru Manoel, Valletta, before touring to Transmediale Berlin, where it was presented in the Haus der Kulturen der Welt. Both presentations sold out. A studio recording of the Berlin performance, captured in Ambisonics for binaural playback, is available as a limited edition release.
