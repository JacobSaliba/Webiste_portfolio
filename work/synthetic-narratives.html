AI-generated storytelling in which the audience co-authors branching narrative structures in real time — authorship as a shared, contested act.

Synthetic Narratives places visitors at a touch interface through which they enter a fragment of a story — a sentence, an image, a sound — and watch a language model extend, contradict, or absorb their contribution into an evolving narrative displayed on a large projection surface. Over the course of an exhibition day, hundreds of contributions accumulate into a living document that no single author controls.

Concept

Who Owns the Story?

The work stages a confrontation between two contested notions of authorship: the romantic ideal of the singular creative voice, and the increasingly prevalent reality of collaborative, distributed, machine-assisted text production. Neither is celebrated nor critiqued — instead, the work holds them in productive tension, making the question visible without resolving it.

The language model is not a tool in this piece but a co-author with recognisable tendencies, preferences, and biases — tendencies that visitors quickly learn to anticipate, subvert, or exploit. The relationship between human and machine input becomes a negotiation, sometimes cooperative, sometimes adversarial.

Process

Narrative Architecture & Interface Design

The narrative engine is built on a fine-tuned language model given a set of structural constraints — genre, tense, maximum deviation from prior contributions — that prevent the narrative from collapsing into incoherence while allowing for significant variation. The interface was designed to be legible without instruction: a single text field and a large, slowly scrolling projection of the accumulating story above it.

Branching points in the narrative are surfaced periodically as multiple-choice moments, allowing the collective to make explicit decisions about the story's direction. These votes are weighted by recency and volume of contribution, so habitual participants carry more influence — a deliberate structural choice that mirrors dynamics of participatory democracy.

Context

Collective Authorship in Practice

At Valletta Contemporary, the installation ran for six weeks. By the end, the accumulated narrative ran to over 80,000 words — a novel-length document authored by several thousand visitors and a language model, in a language that is neither fully human nor fully synthetic. A printed excerpt was produced as a limited edition publication.
