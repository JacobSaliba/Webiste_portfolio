An autonomous installation that responds to viewer density — the crowd becomes the generative engine of its own visual feedback loops.

System Error is a durational installation in which the system watches its audience. A camera array feeds real-time crowd density data into a generative visual engine, which produces and degrades imagery in direct proportion to how many people are present — and how they are distributed across the space. The piece is neither interactive in the conventional sense nor passive: it is systemic. The audience collectively authors an outcome none of them individually controls.

Concept

The System That Watches Back

The title refers simultaneously to computational failure and to the broader failures of systemic thinking — economic, ecological, political — in which individual actors participate in collective outcomes they did not choose and cannot easily reverse. The visual language of the piece enacts this: when few people are present, the imagery is legible and ordered; as density increases, the system is pushed into instability, generating glitch, compression artefacts, and feedback loops that reference the aesthetics of digital error.

The work draws on Norbert Wiener's cybernetics and Humberto Maturana's autopoiesis — the idea that systems define themselves through the maintenance of their own organisation, even as they respond to perturbation from outside. System Error asks: what does a system look like when its perturbation is us?

Process

Computer Vision & Generative Architecture

The technical infrastructure consists of three ceiling-mounted cameras running OpenCV-based body detection, feeding density metrics into a custom Processing sketch that governs the visual output. A secondary system uses Arduino-controlled hardware relays to modulate the physical brightness and frequency of projection, introducing an analog layer of instability into the otherwise digital feedback chain.

The visual engine uses a reaction-diffusion system seeded by the incoming density data — a mathematical model of pattern formation that, at certain parameters, produces recognisable organic forms and at others collapses into noise. The transition between these states is the work's central visual event.

Context

Behaviour & Reception

At Spazju Kreattiv, the installation ran for three weeks in the main gallery, operating continuously during opening hours. Documentation revealed that audiences quickly intuited the system's logic and began experimenting — clustering, dispersing, forming shapes — to push the visual output in different directions. This emergent play was not designed but was entirely consistent with the work's premise.
